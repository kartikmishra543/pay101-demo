1. Purpose and scope

Pay101 (“Pay101”, “we”, “us” or “our”) is committed to preventing misuse of its merchant platform and payment API services for money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, sanctions evasion, or other unlawful activity.

This public policy describes the principles Pay101 applies to merchant onboarding and use of its payment technology. The precise legal duties applicable to Pay101 depend on the services it actually performs, its contractual arrangements, and the laws and regulatory requirements applicable to its legal entity.

This policy applies to merchants, authorised dashboard users, and relevant personnel and service providers to the extent applicable. It should be read alongside the Merchant Agreement, Terms and Conditions, Privacy Policy, and any applicable service-specific requirements.

2. Services and transaction flows

Pay101’s API Integration Documentation v1.0 describes merchant login and token generation, authentication using a Bearer JWT and the X-Authorization-Key and X-Module-Secret headers, PayIn order creation and payment verification, PayOut initiation and status checks, and IP whitelisting.

The documentation describes PayIn collection requests in INR, encrypted PayIn payloads, payout instructions in INR using beneficiary bank details and supported transfer modes (IMPS, NEFT or RTGS), and callback-based status notifications. Merchants must follow the current API documentation and their Merchant Agreement.

This description does not determine whether Pay101 or a third party is the regulated payment service provider, receives or holds funds, or bears a particular statutory obligation. Those matters must be stated accurately in the Merchant Agreement and reviewed by qualified counsel.

3. Merchant due diligence

Before enabling services, and periodically where appropriate, Pay101 may request and review information needed to understand a merchant and its intended use of the platform. This may include legal business name, company registration details, address, contact information, directors and beneficial owners, authorised representatives, tax details, settlement account information, business model, products or services, expected transaction volumes, and supporting documents.

Merchants must provide accurate, current and complete information and promptly notify Pay101 of material changes. Pay101 may request additional evidence or clarification where information is incomplete, inconsistent, or presents an elevated risk.

4. Risk assessment and enhanced checks

Pay101 may assess risk by considering the merchant’s industry, ownership structure, geographic exposure, products and services, expected transaction activity, use of payment partners, adverse information, and other relevant factors.

Where risk is elevated, Pay101 may apply additional checks, request information about source of funds or business activity, require further approval, limit available services, or decline onboarding. Any politically exposed person (PEP), sanctions, or adverse-media screening should be performed only to the extent applicable and supported by Pay101’s actual procedures and legal obligations.

5. Monitoring and suspicious activity

Where appropriate to its role and systems, Pay101 may review transaction information, API activity, payout requests, callbacks, and transaction status records for unusual patterns or potential misuse. Examples of risk indicators may include activity inconsistent with a merchant’s declared business, unusual spikes in volume, repeated failed or reversed transactions, inaccurate beneficiary information, or attempts to evade controls.

Pay101 may seek supporting information, delay or reject a request, or restrict access where permitted by law and the relevant agreement. Where Pay101 is legally required to make a report to a competent authority, it will follow the applicable reporting process.

Pay101 does not represent that it operates a particular monitoring tool, reporting registration, or compliance function unless that is actually in place.

6. Prohibited use

Merchants must not use Pay101 to facilitate money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, sanctions evasion, unlawful transfers, transactions involving prohibited goods or services, identity misuse, or any other unlawful activity. Merchants must not attempt to bypass verification, transaction controls, IP restrictions, or security requirements.

Pay101 may reject an application, request additional information, restrict transactions, suspend an account, or terminate services where permitted or required by applicable law or the relevant agreement.

7. API security and transaction verification

Merchants must protect their merchant login credentials, Bearer JWTs, X-Authorization-Key, X-Module-Secret, AES Key, AES IV, and Transaction PIN (TPIN). Credentials must not be exposed in public code, browser-side JavaScript, URLs, or public repositories. Merchants must use secure server-side storage, restrict access to authorised personnel, rotate or revoke compromised credentials, and follow Pay101’s IP-whitelisting requirements.

For PayIn, the API documentation instructs merchants to verify payment status with Pay101 after receiving a callback and not to credit a customer solely on the basis of callback data. For PayOut, merchants should use the documented status endpoint and must not mark a payout successful until the API confirms SUCCESS. Merchants remain responsible for implementing the documented verification flow safely.

8. Records, confidentiality and cooperation

Pay101 may retain merchant verification records, transaction references, API/security logs, and compliance-related information for as long as necessary for service delivery, security, dispute handling, and applicable legal obligations. Retention periods must be established in accordance with the laws that apply to Pay101 and must not be assumed from this policy alone.

Information may be disclosed to service providers, payment partners, regulators, law enforcement, or other recipients where necessary and lawful. Access should be limited to authorised persons and appropriate security measures should be used.

9. Review and contact

Pay101 may update this policy to reflect changes in its services, risks, or applicable requirements. The current version should be published on the website.

For questions about this policy or to report suspected misuse, email support@pay101.io. Include a transaction reference if available. Keep passwords, PINs and API credentials out of email.

For the Indian regulatory framework, see the RBI Master Direction on Regulation of Payment Aggregators and the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002. The requirements that apply depend on the entity and the activities it performs.
